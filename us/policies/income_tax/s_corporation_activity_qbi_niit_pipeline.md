## Outcome

The direct-authored draft demonstrates the intended behavior for a bounded
one-owner/one-activity slice that derives both QBI and NIIT inputs from the
same activity row without collapsing their legal predicates. It admits only a
100%-owned S corporation in tax year 2026.
Partnerships, rentals, trading businesses, negative ordinary income, partial
ownership, other entity forms, and post-2026 periods fail closed.

The slice keeps section 469 in the standalone
`us/statutes/26/469/c/1.yaml` module. Both downstream legs consume its
owner-specific passive predicate:

- section 1411 includes ordinary business income in NIIT only when the
  activity is passive with respect to this owner; and
- section 199A(i)(2)(B) treats QBI as active only when the same owner
  materially participates within the meaning of section 469(h).

Participation does not alter section 199A(c)-(f) item classification or the
pre-minimum deduction. It can alter the final post-OBBBA deduction when the
section 199A(i) floor binds. SSTB status is invariant below the QBI threshold
and affects QBI in and above the phase-in range, but it never determines NIIT
passivity.

## Draft behavioral boundaries

The positive domain requires exactly one relation row with:

- entity code `2` (S corporation);
- ownership share exactly `1`;
- an explicit operating section 162 trade-or-business fact;
- nonrental, nontrading, and non-PTP activity;
- nonnegative owner-allocable ordinary income included in taxable income;
- explicit SSTB and section 469 material-participation facts;
- explicit qualified-item, W-2-wage, and UBIA facts; and
- no unresolved QBI loss/owner adjustment, NIIT recharacterization, grouping,
  former-passive, working-interest, section 1401(b), section 911, or unrelated
  income-category boundary.

Completed-return taxable income and AGI remain inputs and must already include
the activity's ordinary income exactly once. The slice does not construct a
return or duplicate the taxable-income spine.
